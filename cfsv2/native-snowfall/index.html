How the estimate is made

Native CFSv2 surface snowfall rate (SRWEQ, kg m⁻² s⁻¹) is integrated over each calendar month and averaged across the same 24 forecast cycles. Snowfall water equivalent is multiplied by the published CIPS mean snow-to-liquid ratio for each NWS forecast office area (CWA). JFM is the sum of January, February and March. These are accumulated snowfall estimates, not standing snowpack depth.

The ratios are pooled 1971–2000 event means, not monthly values or predicted storm ratios. The source sample uses snowfall greater than 2 inches and liquid equivalent greater than 0.11 inch. Applying those ratios to all model snowfall is an approximation. Native input does not remove model bias or establish forecast skill.

Coverage, colors and downloads

97 of 116 CONUS CWAs have verified ratios; the remaining 19 are hatched. Historical ratios use current same-code CWA boundaries, which can create artificial steps. No extra smoothing or contour lines were added. Colors saturate at 200 inches; larger values are retained in the numerical grid.

The map uses bilinear sampling of native water equivalent followed by exact CWA assignment. This does not add meteorological resolution. Downloads retain the original model grid with a CWA ratio assigned at each grid point, so a coarse cell can differ from nearby display pixels at a CWA boundary. Missing values remain missing.
